7 Restaurant Mistakes to Avoid in Scottsdale

By Volanti Scottsdale · Updated October 25, 2026

The most common mistakes when choosing a restaurant in Scottsdale involve misjudging dining timing, overlooking venue layout logistics, failing to plan for peak group reservations, and ignoring specialized menu options. Selecting the right dining experience requires verifying exact operational hours, checking party size requirements, understanding seating environments, and confirming specific dietary preferences prior to arrival.

Making a dining reservation or planning a major gathering should be a seamless experience. Whether you are seeking a casual weekday happy hour or arranging a large-scale corporate function, avoiding routine planning pitfalls ensures your time at the table is memorable for the right reasons. Below are seven critical restaurant mistakes diners make in the Scottsdale area and how to avoid them.

1. Assuming Weekend Hours Apply to Midweek Dining

One of the most frequent errors guests make is showing up for a specific meal service without verifying weekday operational schedules. Many upscale and experiential restaurants maintain distinct hours for lunch, dinner, and weekend brunch rather than running a single continuous service.

To avoid disappointment, always confirm the precise service windows for the day you plan to visit. For instance, Volanti Scottsdale menus reflect distinct dining windows across the week:

  • Lunch: Tuesday through Friday from 11 AM to 3 PM
  • Dinner: Tuesday through Thursday from 4:30 PM to 9 PM, and Friday & Saturday from 4:30 PM to 10 PM
  • Brunch: Saturday & Sunday from 9 AM to 3 PM
  • Happy Hour: Weekdays from 3 PM to 6 PM

Checking these timeframes in advance prevents arriving during an afternoon kitchen closure or missing out on midday menu items.

2. Trying to Walk In with Large Parties Without Calling

Assuming a restaurant can accommodate a group of eight, ten, or twenty people without prior notification often leads to long wait times or split seating. While walk-ins are routinely welcome for small tables, large groups require precise floor management and kitchen coordination.

When hosting a larger group, check the venue’s policy on group size thresholds. At Volanti Scottsdale, walk-ins are welcome for standard parties, but guests with a party of 8 or more are requested to call ahead at 480.657.2426. Calling ahead ensures the hospitality team can prepare appropriate seating in advance, preserving the flow of your gathering.

3. Confusing General Dining Areas with Dedicated Event Spaces

A common mistake when organizing a celebration, corporate gathering, or fundraiser is attempting to fit a large crowd into standard dining room reservations rather than reserving a dedicated event space. Standard restaurant tables are designed for intimate meals, while corporate dinners, birthdays, and major events require appropriate capacity, tailored layouts, and customized menu handling.

Understanding venue options helps you match your guest count to the proper space:

Event Type & Size Typical Event Goal Venue Consideration Recommended Option
Small Group (Up to 15) Birthdays, social gatherings Open-air setting with casual atmosphere Outdoor Patio
Medium Group (Up to 200) Dinners, corporate banquets Private indoor room with structured seating Thunderbird & Stearman Rooms

Choosing the right physical space ensures adequate room for movement, specialized service staff, and clear views without crowding standard restaurant operations.

4. Overlooking Unique On-Site Experiences and Add-Ons

Guests often reserve a simple table without exploring the broader suite of experiences a venue offers. Many destination restaurants provide curated activities, private storage programs, or specialized transportation packages that transform a standard meal into a memorable event.

When planning a special evening in Scottsdale, research special offerings beyond the standard dinner menu. For example, guests can explore curated Volanti experiences such as:

  • Helicopter Flight Packages: Combining a three-course lunch, brunch, or dinner with a private flight over the Valley.
  • Sommelier-Curated Wine Lockers: Private on-site wine storage offering members exclusive benefits and access to select vintages.
  • Off-Site & Private Aviation Catering: Bringing chef-driven culinary offerings directly to aircraft through dedicated jet catering services.

Failing to investigate these options beforehand means missing out on customized date-night or VIP entertainment opportunities.

5. Expecting standard Bar Menus During Peak Sunset and Flight Times

Arriving for evening drinks during peak hours without knowing the specific menu offerings or seating arrangements can result in missing out on popular featured dishes and drink specials.

If you are visiting specifically for a sunset or happy hour experience, plan to sit in designated lounge or patio areas where full cocktail menus and small bites are featured. Reviewing the menu options ahead of time allows you to sample signature dishes—such as Short Rib Tacos, Volanti Meatballs, Tiny Tuna Tartare, BBQ Pork Sliders, or the Take Off Trio with house focaccia—alongside specialty cocktails, house highballs, local craft beer, or select wines.

6. Arriving for Experiential Packages Without Leaving Arrival Buffers

When booking packages that involve scheduled departure times—such as a flight experience paired with a meal—arriving precisely on time is actually arriving late. Experiential dining requires check-in procedures, safety briefings, or transitional walk-times before the scheduled activity begins.

For paired experiences, always build in a minimum 15-minute arrival window prior to your scheduled time slot. Arriving early allows the team to complete check-in smoothly at the venue, transfer you comfortably to the activity staging area, and ensure everything stays on schedule.

7. Ignoring Parking and Building Access Instructions

Few things ruin the start of a meal faster than getting lost in a multi-building complex or struggling to find parking. Dinners located inside mixed-use, aviation, or commercial centers often have specific parking lots and entrance access rules that differ from standard standalone retail strip malls.

Before setting out, review location and entry details:

  • Address: Locate the exact building address—such as 15000 North Airport Drive in Scottsdale, Arizona.
  • Building Level: Confirm which floor the dining room occupies (for instance, the second floor of the Aviation Business Center).
  • Security Requirements: Check whether security credentials or passes are needed ( dining above the runway at Scottsdale Airport requires no boarding pass or security checkpoint).
  • Parking: Identify designated parking areas, such as covered parking situated directly across from the main building entrance.
