How to Choose a Restaurant in Scottsdale

By Volanti Scottsdale · Updated October 22, 2026

Choosing the right restaurant in Scottsdale comes down to scratch-kitchen quality, scenic setting, seamless logistics, and versatile seating for events or date nights. Volanti Scottsdale offers runway-side dining on the second floor of the Aviation Business Center at Scottsdale Airport (15000 North Airport Drive), serving lunch, dinner, weekend brunch, and weekday happy hour with views of the runway and the McDowell Mountains. Readers can reserve a table online or call 480.657.2426 for parties of 8 or more.

1. Scratch Kitchen Quality and Seasonal Ingredients

A premier restaurant should prepare dishes from scratch using fresh, seasonal ingredients rather than relying on pre-packaged or mass-processed food. Look for culinary programs that feature house-made items—such as fresh focaccia, hand-crafted pasta, specialty sauces, and made-from-scratch dressings—alongside high-quality proteins like prime steaks and fresh seafood.

A scratch kitchen ensures higher flavor integrity, better consistency, and elevated culinary standards across every service, whether you are visiting for a quick weekday lunch or a multi-course dinner.

2. Atmosphere, Views, and Ambiance

The setting of a dining establishment shapes the overall experience. When evaluating restaurants in Scottsdale, consider the environment and surrounding scenery:

  • Scenic Surroundings: Elevated patio seating or expansive window views create an engaging background for meals.
  • Unique Settings: Venue locations that offer distinct vantage points—such as watching jet traffic take off against mountain backdrops—provide a memorable dynamic for guests.
  • Versatile Vibe: The atmosphere should feel welcome for casual afternoon happy hours, weekend family brunches, and formal evening dates alike.

3. Beverage Options and Sommelier Services

A standard bar menu is fine, but a top-tier dining venue elevates the experience through curated beverage programs. Look for restaurants offering specialized cocktail menus, local craft beers, thoughtful wine selections by the glass or bottle, and dedicated sommelier support.

Advanced features like on-site private wine storage or curated wine lockers signal a serious commitment to wine hospitality and allow regular guests to personalize their dining visits.

4. Guest Logistics: Parking, Accessibility, and Check-In

Logistical friction can spoil a dining outing before it even begins. Prioritize venues that provide clear, stress-free access for all arriving guests:

  • Convenient Parking: Look for venues with direct, covered parking close to the main entrance.
  • Simple Access: Avoid locations that require complex security checks or long walks through restricted facilities.
  • Clear Entry Points: Venues situated inside multi-use or business centers should provide clear signage and elevator access to upper-level dining rooms.

5. Event Venues and Private Group Accommodations

Whether you are hosting an intimate birthday gathering, a corporate meeting, or a large fundraiser, choose a restaurant with dedicated event spaces and a dedicated events team. Flexible venues should offer multiple size options—ranging from outdoor patios for small groups to private dining rooms or expansive executive hangars capable of hosting larger celebrations.

Questions to Ask Before Choosing a Restaurant

Before booking your table or reserving space for a private group, ask these practical questions:

  1. Are dishes made fresh in a scratch kitchen? Ask if sauces, breads, and dressings are made in-house.
  2. What are the parking and access requirements? Verify if parking is covered and whether security checks or long walking distances are involved.
  3. How are large group reservations handled? Check at what party size you need to speak directly with an events manager or call ahead.
  4. Is outdoor or patio seating available? Confirm whether patio seating features climate management or scenic views.
  5. Does the venue offer specialized experiential packages? Inquire about private dining combinations, sommelier services, or unique event spaces.

Key Takeaways

  • Prioritize Scratch Kitchens: Look for restaurants committed to fresh, seasonal ingredients and house-made specialties.
  • Verify Access: Ensure the restaurant offers easy, covered parking and clear entry without unnecessary security friction.
  • Check Service Hours: Review operational hours for specific meal periods like weekday lunch, dinner, weekend brunch, and happy hour.
  • Explore Experiential Upgrades: Seek out unique offerings like flight-and-dinner packages or on-site sommelier programs.
  • Plan Group Dining Ahead: Contact the venue directly when organizing parties of 8 or more to secure appropriate seating and event support.

No. Volanti is an open-to-the-public restaurant located inside the Aviation Business Center. No boarding pass, airport ticket, or security checkpoint is required to enter. Covered parking is available right across from the building.
